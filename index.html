// Findings

## Atmospheric Reality Gap

Your copy declares "We do things right, and we do right things" — an employee-owned firm built on craft, collaboration, and accountability. That is a premium positioning statement. What the image infrastructure signals is something measurably cheaper: 6 of 7 content images carry zero alt text, and one of the featured project images is filed as `featured-image.jpg` — a placeholder name that predates any actual project decision. No hero images were submitted for this audit, so no aesthetic judgment is being made here. What the forensics *do* confirm is that the visual assets supporting the Salish Lodge Renovation and Sound Transit Northgate Station projects — two of your three featured marquee projects — are named with timestamps and defaults rather than anything that connects to Absher's identity or the projects themselves. A high-value owner's rep comparing GCs reads that infrastructure signal as a firm that hasn't invested in telling its own story. That gap between the copy's confidence and the asset discipline underneath it costs you credibility before a buyer clicks a single project page.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

Your Facebook link — one of only two social proof pathways in the footer — returns a 400 error. That is a dead door on a page where a buyer is already evaluating whether Absher is active, current, and worth a call. The broken URL (`https://www.facebook.com/Absher-Construction-Company-173979772644526/`) has likely been wrong long enough to be baked into the site template, which tells a senior facilities director one thing: nobody is walking this buyer journey from the outside. The featured project carousel loops the same three projects — WSU Elson S. Floyd, Salish Lodge, Northgate Station — with images uploaded in December 2018. If your most compelling current work lives only behind a "View More" click that most buyers won't take, the homepage is arguing *against* your portfolio, not for it. A competitor whose homepage rotates recent, well-documented project photography wins the first impression on delivery capability even if their actual work is comparable.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Your page title is six characters: "Absher." That is not a metadata strategy — it is an empty field with a logo in it. There is no meta description and no `og:image`, which means every LinkedIn share, every Slack paste of your URL, and every email where someone forwards your site to a decision-maker renders as a blank card. You rank #1 organically for "Absher Construction Company" — a branded search term people use when they already know you exist. That ranking protects nothing when the metadata provides zero copy for a buyer who hasn't decided yet. The missing `og:image` alone is forfeiting visual real estate on every social share your team, your clients, and your subcontractors generate. Meanwhile, `IMG_9121.jpg` — a news section image — is untagged and unsearchable. At 86% alt-text failure across 7 content images, your project photography is invisible to image search at exactly the moment a prospective owner is looking for a GC whose finished work looks like something they want to build.
